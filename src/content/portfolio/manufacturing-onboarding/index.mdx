## The Challenge

Sandy Systems LLC had two converging problems that would uproot their learning ecosystem in the next five years.
 - Tenured subject matter experts were reaching retirement age, threatening a massive loss of knowledge
 - The current onboarding for new employees was informal leading to preventable knowledge gaps

The lack of cross training for sales and customer support led to inconsistant customer experiences depending on the application of the product purchased.

For example, Customer A who purchased precipitated silica to use in powder coatings for metal would receive a higher level of customer service and support than Customer B who purchased the same precipitated silica for use in toothpaste.


## Discovery

Through conversations and needs analysis, we uncovered that 40–60% of the time, customer questions were being fielded by the experts themselves rather than being answered by sales/customer support staff first and then escalated to experts.
Oftentimes sales/customer support staff would rely on the expertise and through the informal onboarding, bad habits had emerged and been passed down to slow the expert-level knowledge building.


## Design Principles

Throughout the process I used a single criterion to guide the project: will this help build expertise. The root problem that Sandy Systems was experiencing wasn't turnover or retirement—it was that no one was actively working towards replacing the current experts. Succession planning.
The problem identified that the employee onboarding process was informal stunted the initial growth and identification of employees who would excel in certain areas and be excellent candidates for expert-level cultivation.
The problem that many of the current experts were approaching retirement added an expiration date for their current system.

What was actually needed wasn't just a structured onboarding or knowledge capture system. It was a clear pathway from new hire to expert.

## Tradeoffs

Designing the solution had to take into account very real constraints. First being that the ongoing maintenance of the program had to be handed over to the learning enablement team at Sandy Systems.
This meant that a templated approach had to be taken so the learning team could reliably reproduce additional courses and content.
This also meant that all the developed assets had to be standardized and developed for the lowest-common denominator. All courses and content needed to be easily editable in either Microsoft Word or directly in Articulate Storyline.
Articulate Rise was the first desired platform, but there was no easy way to share source files to include those in the delivery, so many of the Rise-like interactions were recreated in Storyline to use as a template.
